# CVSmith CV house style

These rules decide how CV text is *improved*. They never apply to parsing: parsing copies the
candidate's wording as written, and every improvement is shown to the candidate to accept or
reject.

## Writing rules

1. Use concise professional language.
2. Prefer active verbs and strong action words ("Led", "Delivered", "Reduced").
3. Focus on outcomes, ownership, scale and impact.
4. Remove vague phrases and unnecessary repetition.
5. Avoid first-person pronouns: "Led the migration", not "I led the migration".
6. Do not use decorative language unsupported by evidence ("world-class", "visionary").
7. Preserve accurate job titles and employment dates exactly as given.
8. Use terminology appropriate to the candidate's profession.
9. Expand uncommon abbreviations on first use when the meaning is known; otherwise leave them as written.
10. Keep sentences concise; split long sentences into separate bullet points.
11. Never add facts that are not in the candidate's material.
12. Replace weak phrasing when the facts show what the candidate did: "Responsible for", "helped with", "assisted with", "worked on" and "involved in" become the verb that says it. "Responsible for maintaining the payments API" becomes "Maintained the payments API". When the facts do not show it, keep the weaker verb.
13. Remove decorative adjectives from summaries that the facts do not support, such as "highly motivated", "passionate", "results-driven" or "dynamic". If no fact is left, return the summary unchanged.
14. An objective summary says what the candidate wants (for example "Seeking a role...", "Looking for..." or "Aiming to..."). Replace it with a factual statement from the CV: who the candidate is, their role and main achievements. Use no first person. If the CV has no facts to state, leave the summary unchanged.
15. Start each job or project bullet with an action verb, in the past tense for past roles and the present
    tense for the current role, when the facts show what the candidate did. Prefer one of these verbs when it
    says the same thing: Accelerated, Achieved, Administered, Advised, Analysed, Architected, Assessed, Audited, Automated, Built, Coached, Collaborated, Conducted, Configured, Consolidated, Coordinated, Created, Cut, Defined, Delivered, Deployed, Designed, Developed, Directed, Documented, Drove, Eliminated, Engineered, Established, Evaluated, Executed, Expanded, Facilitated, Forecast, Generated, Grew, Identified, Implemented, Improved, Increased, Initiated, Integrated, Introduced, Launched, Led, Maintained, Managed, Mentored, Migrated, Modernised, Monitored, Negotiated, Onboarded, Optimised, Orchestrated, Organised, Oversaw, Owned, Partnered, Piloted, Planned, Prepared, Presented, Processed, Produced, Reconciled, Recruited, Redesigned, Reduced, Resolved, Restructured, Reviewed, Saved, Scaled, Secured, Simplified, Spearheaded, Standardised, Streamlined, Supervised, Tested, Trained, Transformed, Validated, Wrote. Keep the meaning and scope: never turn a supporting role into ownership
    ("Supported" does not become "Led"); if ownership is unclear, keep the verb that is there.
16. Aim for bullets of 15 to 30 words, one achievement each. Split a longer bullet into two. Join two very
    short bullets that describe the same work, or write a short bullet out in full, using only facts already in
    the same description. Never add a fact, number or tool to reach the length: if there are too few facts, leave
    the bullet short.

For a finished document (PDF or Word), also keep it ATS-readable: real text only, one reading
order, no text inside images, graphics or tables.

## Applying the rules

- **Facts are fixed, wording is not.** Rewrite how something is said, never what happened.
  Employers, titles, dates, degrees, numbers, clients and tools come from the candidate only.
- **No invented numbers.** If an outcome has no figure, leave it without one. Do not add a
  number, a placeholder, or a question asking the candidate for one.
- **Abbreviations:** if the meaning is known (a standard term like ERP, or spelled out
  elsewhere in the CV), expand it automatically on first use. If it is not known or could mean
  several things (e.g. "OWS", "HDR"), leave it exactly as written. Never ask the candidate.
- **Keep meaning and scope.** "Supported the migration" must not become "Led the migration".
  If ownership is unclear, keep the weaker verb.
- **An existing summary keeps its content.** It may be a positioning statement for the role
  the candidate is aiming for rather than an account of their past, so improve only its
  wording: keep its intent and claims, and add nothing. Write a new summary only when there is
  none, from the candidate's experience in the rest of the CV.
- **Re-ordering is allowed** as a suggestion: sections (e.g. projects before work experience
  for a fresher) or bullets (strongest outcome first).

## Suggestion format

For each field or bullet, give:

- **Current:** the text as it is now
- **Suggested:** the improved text

Do not explain which rule was applied or ask the candidate questions; the suggestion speaks for
itself.

If the current text already follows the rules, say so and suggest nothing.

## Examples

Generic examples; never copy real candidates' details into this file.

| Current | Suggested |
|---|---|
| I was responsible for managing a team that moved around 120 customer reports to the new dashboard tool within the deadline. | Led the move of ~120 customer reports to the new dashboard tool, on schedule. |
| Successfully worked on various projects using different technologies in the retail domain. | Worked on retail technology projects. |
| Highly accomplished, results-driven visionary leader. | (No suggestion: there is nothing factual to keep. Leave it to the candidate.) |
| Built the QMT module for the client's ERP rollout, which included testing it and also writing the user guides and also training 40 users. | Built the QMT module for the client's ERP (enterprise resource planning) rollout. • Tested the module and wrote the user guides. • Trained 40 users. |
| Improved the onboarding process for new joiners. | (Unchanged: no figure is given, so none is added.) |

Notes on the examples: "Led" is used only because the CV says the candidate managed the team;
ERP is expanded because the expansion is certain, QMT is not.
